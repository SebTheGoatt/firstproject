The Five-Paragraph Plan

Every military operation starts with the same five paragraphs: where we stand, what we're doing, how, what keeps us going, and who's in charge. It works just as well for paying off a card, getting back in shape, or starting a new chapter. Fill in the worksheet and your order writes itself.

1Where you standSituation

Ground truth first. A plan built on how you wish things were falls apart on day three.

In an order this is the enemy. Name it so you can plan around it.
2The missionMission

One sentence: who, what, when, where and why. If you can't say it in one breath, it's two missions.

3How you'll do itExecution

Intent is what "done" looks like, so you can adapt when the plan breaks. Phases keep it from feeling like one giant hill.

Commander's intent.
Contingencies. Decide now, while you're calm.
4What keeps you goingSustainment

Most plans don't fail on effort. They run out of fuel, money or support.

If things get heavy, you don't have to carry it alone. In the US, call or text 988 any time. Veterans and service members can press 1 after dialing to reach the Veterans Crisis Line.

5Who's in charge and how you'll check inCommand & signal

You're in command. Pick a second in command who'll ask how it's going, and set the time you'll report.

Accountability partner. A first name is plenty.
The question you'll ask yourself on a hard day, and the answer you've already decided on.
