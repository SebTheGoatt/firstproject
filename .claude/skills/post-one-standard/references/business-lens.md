# The business lens

How to judge an idea for Post One the way the owner would. It draws on their training (marketing, strategy,
finance, business analysis, legal and ethical issues, AI in business, SEO and e-commerce) and on what Post One has
learned so far. Use it in step 3 of the standard; quote the one or two questions that decided the call.

## 1. Build the line (distribution)

- "Anyone can make the tacos. Not everyone has the line." Building is cheap now; customers are the scarce thing. If
  the site already covers an idea, the useful answer is how to get the next 5 paid bookings, not more code.
- One kind of person, one problem: a busy parent in Spring who wants the lawn handled on a set day; a senior in
  Colony Ridge who can't mow anymore. Write for that person in their own words (read local Facebook groups and
  Nextdoor for how they complain: no-shows, surprise prices).
- Sell before you build: test demand with door hangers and posts (Marketing hub QR codes are tagged per channel),
  then build for what sold.
- Own the list: followers are rented. Repeat customers and their texts are the list; never put promotions in texts
  (SMS consent covers booking updates only).
- Search and AI assistants: verified Search Console and Bing, the sitemap, real area pages, `/llms.txt`, FAQ data,
  and consistent listings (Google, Bing Places, Apple, Yelp, Nextdoor).

## 2. Keep them (retention)

- Homejoy kept ~25% of customers after month one. Repeat booking in one tap, a set day, upfront locked prices,
  "on the way" texts and done photos are the answer. Anything that adds surprise (price changes on site, missed
  days) works against it.

## 3. Time and margin (operations and finance)

- One area a day keeps driving short; the route builder drops off-area stops. Ideas that scatter the crew cost more
  than they earn.
- Pay first, then mow: no cash, no unpaid card on the route.
- Unit economics: base $75 before tax (the local market quotes ~$35–$55), so the pitch is reliability, not price. A
  discount or giveaway must say who pays for it. Nothing is free: giving is the cause share, the round-up and paid
  sponsorships.
- Prefer keyless, free, CORS-open data (NOAA, OSRM, Overture, OpenFreeMap) over accounts and monthly fees.

## 4. Risk (legal and ethical)

- No claim we can't back up: insurance, background checks, ratings and stats appear only when settings say so.
- Privacy: no personal data in the public repo or on public pages; photos stripped of location data.
- Texas d/b/a: the legal name (Semper Green LLC) stays in the footer and policies until the assumed-name filing.
- Refund and rain rules are published terms; changes go to the owner first.
- AI output is a draft: it uses live facts from the app (`business_facts()`), never invented ones.

## 5. Upkeep (strategy)

- Do fewer things well: mowing first, add-ons second, no expansion beyond our areas (Exec failed by doing too much).
- Every page and feature is something to maintain and translate. If it doesn't help the line, retention, time or
  risk, it's a "not now".

## Verdict words

Adopt · Adapt (say what was left out) · Not now (name the trigger) · Skip · No code needed (give the plan).
